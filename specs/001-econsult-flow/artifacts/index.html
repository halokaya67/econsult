Flow record · 001-econsult-flow

How this feature was built

The process record of the e-consult submission flow, copied into the repository so every page opens from a clone with no network and no server.

What this record is

The feature was built with a gated, agent-driven implementation flow. In order: platform research; a snapshot of how the app worked before the change; a spec that went through five checker rounds; a plan that went through three; then twenty-two steps executed one at a time, each verified with the project's own commands, and for the six steps that added a screen, previewed on a simulator before the step was allowed to pass. After the last step a final checker reviewed the whole change over three rounds, and the finished app was walked through end to end on a phone simulator and a tablet simulator.

A step passed on its own only when its checks were green, its simulator preview was clean and it stayed inside the files it was allowed to touch. Anything else — a failing check, a visual defect, a file outside the fence — stopped the flow for a decision, and the decision is part of the record below.

At a glance

  • 22 steps planned, executed and committed.
  • 11 checker rounds: five on the spec, three on the plan, three on the finished change.
  • 13 simulator runs behind the six steps that added a screen — first runs plus the re-checks that confirmed each fix.
  • 16 scenarios in the finish-phase walkthrough, all passing: ten on an iPhone 17 simulator, six on an iPad Pro 11-inch — including an Accessibility Inspector audit that returned no warnings on any of the five states it audited.

Reading notes

  • Every link is relative. Open index.html from the file system and the whole record works offline; the web fonts and the diagram renderer are the only things that want a connection, and the pages read fine without them.
  • The screenshots are downscaled copies of the originals — long edge at most 900 px — so the record stays small enough to live in the repository.
  • The checker reports and the two lane reports are rendered from the markdown the flow wrote at the time. The wording and the verdicts are theirs, unedited.
